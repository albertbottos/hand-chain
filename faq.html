Hand Chain FAQ

Short answers to the questions that come up most before a first hand chain purchase, grouped by fit, wear and care.

Updated 24 November 2026

01 Naming

What is a hand chain called?

It is most often sold as a hand chain, and sometimes as a ring bracelet or hand bracelet. The older term "slave bracelet" still appears in search results and in some listings, but most current collections use hand chain.

02 Versus bracelet

Is a hand chain the same as a bracelet?

No. A bracelet is a closed loop around the wrist. A hand chain adds a ring and a chain across the back of the hand, connecting the two. That connection is what keeps the piece from sliding, and it is also what makes the sizing more complicated.

03 Ring finger

Which finger does the ring go on?

Index, middle or ring finger all work. The ring finger is the most common because it is the easiest to size and the chain sits in a straight line to the wrist. Most people wear the piece on the non-dominant hand, since typing and writing put pressure on a chain that crosses the hand.

04 Sizing

How do I know what size hand chain to buy?

You need three measurements: wrist circumference, ring size, and the span across the back of your hand from the base of the finger to the wrist. Ordering by ring size alone is the usual cause of a bad fit. The sizing guide walks through all three.

05 Daily wear

Can I wear a hand chain every day?

Yes, with two conditions. Choose a construction that can take it, which usually means a single strand and a metal that will not wear through quickly, and size the bracelet so it does not pull against the ring. Take the piece off for swimming, showering and the gym.

06 Stretching

Will a hand chain stretch over time?

The chain itself does not stretch much, but the points that move wear: the clasp, the jump rings and, on plated pieces, the surface where the chain rubs the ring. That wear shows as a looser clasp or a lighter patch of color rather than a longer chain.

07 Adjusting

Can I shorten or lengthen a hand chain?

Usually yes. Many pieces include an extender or a slide closure that covers an inch or two. A jeweler can shorten a chain or add a link, but do not cut it yourself: the ring end is attached in a way that is easy to ruin. If the bracelet is too tight, add an extender rather than removing chain.

08 Metal choice

What metal should I choose?

For frequent wear, gold filled or sterling silver hold up better than plating. Plating is the cheapest option and fine for occasional wear, but a hand chain puts friction on the chain at the ring and the clasp, which is where plating wears first. Solid gold is the most durable and the most expensive. The materials guide explains the labels.

09 Tangling

How do I stop a hand chain tangling with my bracelet?

Wear the hand chain on the opposite wrist from a watch or a heavy bracelet. On the same wrist, keep the bracelet side of the hand chain fine and fasten the hand chain last so its chain sits on top. Multi-strand designs tangle more than single-strand ones, so start there if tangling is a problem.

10 With a watch

Can I wear a hand chain with a watch?

Yes, and the simplest arrangement is one on each wrist. On the same wrist, the two clasps end up in the same place and rub against each other, which wears both pieces faster. If you want them together, choose a very fine bracelet section on the hand chain.

11 Adjustable

Do hand chains come in adjustable sizes?

Many do. Adjustable pieces use a slide closure or an extender on the bracelet, which covers a range of wrist sizes. The ring is usually fixed, so ring size still has to be right, or the ring has to be one that can be resized. The types guide marks which constructions are adjustable.
