How to Measure Hand Chain Size at Home

Updated 24 November 2026

A hand chain is sized in three places at once, which is why it is the jewelry format where guessing goes wrong most often. The bracelet has to fit your wrist, the ring has to fit your finger, and the chain has to span the back of the hand without pulling or hanging loose.

You need a flexible tape measure, or a strip of paper and a ruler, a pen, and a ring that already fits the finger you plan to use. Measure in the evening rather than first thing in the morning: fingers and wrists change size slightly through the day, so an evening measurement is the more forgiving one.

Step one: wrist circumference

Wrap the tape around your wrist just below the wrist bone, where a bracelet naturally settles, and note the measurement.

Add a little room for movement: a chain bracelet should be able to slide about half an inch over the wrist bone. If you are between two bracelet sizes, take the larger one on a hand chain, because a bracelet that is too tight pulls against the ring every time you bend your hand.

Step two: ring size

Take a ring that fits the intended finger comfortably and measure its inner diameter across the widest point, in millimetres. That figure is the number a ring size chart uses. If you do not own a ring that fits, wrap paper around the base of the finger, mark where it overlaps, and measure the length flat to get the circumference, then match it to a chart.

Measure at the base of the finger, not over the knuckle. A ring that is tight at the knuckle will feel tighter once a chain pulls on it.

Step three: the span across the hand

This is the measurement most people skip. Lay your hand flat, palm down. Measure from the base of the finger that will wear the ring, straight across the back of the hand, to the point on the wrist where the bracelet sits. Keep the tape flat rather than following the curve of the hand.

That distance, plus a small allowance for movement, is the chain length the piece needs between the ring and the bracelet. On an average adult hand it lands around three to four inches, but hand width varies a lot, which is why the measurement matters more than the average.

Checking the fit before you order

Compare your three measurements with the product's stated measurements rather than its size label, since labels vary between makers. If a piece has an adjustable closure or an extender, confirm the range it covers and check that your span falls inside it.

Then do a dry run. Hold a piece of string from the base of your finger to your wrist, at the length you measured, and move your hand: make a fist, spread your fingers, rotate your wrist. If the string pulls tight at any point, add half an inch. If it hangs in a loop clear of the hand, take half an inch off.

If you fall between sizes

Size up rather than down on the bracelet, and keep the ring exact. A bracelet that is slightly loose can be adjusted with an extender or a slide closure, while a bracelet that is too tight cannot be fixed without changing the chain tension on the hand. On the ring, a half size either way is usually tolerable if the band is slim; a ring that is too large will rotate and drag the chain out of line.

When to remeasure

Remeasure if your weight has changed or if you are buying for a different hand. Multi-strand and multi-ring pieces need more slack than a single chain, because each additional strand takes up length across the hand.
